# Data: sources, real-time conventions and audit

All inputs are free, public and small (a few MB). `scripts/fetch_data.py fetch`
downloads them and records each response in `data/provenance/raw_manifest.jsonl`
(URL, UTC retrieval time, HTTP status, server `Last-Modified`, SHA-256).
`freeze` transcribes them, without modelling decisions, into `data/frozen`;
`data/provenance/freeze_report.json` summarises the checks. Numbers below refer
to the freeze with cutoff 2026-11-20. Counts of files, gaps, rewrites and
calendar entries come from the freeze; statistics of forecast errors and the
checks of archived observation files come from the feasibility audit of
2026-11-20 and will be regenerated by the C++ pipeline (milestones M1–M2).

## Sources

| Frozen table | Source | Content |
|---|---|---|
| `noaa_forecasts.csv` | CPC `daily_forecasts_7day/YYYY/MM/DD/UtilityGas.Heating.txt` | 7-day HDD forecasts weighted by utility-gas heating customers, 9 Census divisions + CONUS, one row per issue date and region, `d0` = issue day |
| `noaa_forecast_files.csv` | same | one row per calendar day: HTTP status, parse status, `Last-Modified`, posting class |
| `noaa_observed.csv` | CPC `daily_data/YYYY/UtilityGas.Heating.txt` | observed daily HDD, same regions and weights |
| `noaa_regions.csv` | CPC `daily_data/regions/CensusDivisions.txt` | division identifiers and names |
| `eia_storage_five_region.csv` | EIA `ngshistory.xls` | latest-vintage working gas by region and Lower 48, weeks from 2010 |
| `eia_storage_three_region.csv` | EIA `archngshistory.xls` | latest vintage of the discontinued three-region structure, 1993 to 2015-11-06 |
| `eia_storage_original.csv` | EIA `archrevisions.xls`, `revisions.xls` | original estimates prior to revision, with EIA's explanation of each revision |
| `eia_release_exceptions.csv` | archived copies of `ir.eia.gov/ngs/schedule.html` (Wayback Machine) and the live page | holiday release dates and times, mapped to the reference week |

## Timing conventions

- **NOAA bulletin.** Header `Issued 0000UTC YYYYMMDD`; the file is posted on
  the CPC server at about 15:00 UTC (10:00 Eastern) on the issue day. It covers
  the issue day and the following six days, i.e. Friday to Thursday for a
  Friday issue: exactly the storage week, up to the 9-hour offset between
  calendar days and the 09:00 Central gas day.
- **NOAA observations** for day `D` appear at about 08:07 UTC on `D+2`
  (checked on archived copies of the `latest/` file), so on Friday evening
  observations are known up to Wednesday.
- **EIA stocks** refer to 09:00 Central time on Friday (Form EIA-912) and are
  released on the following Thursday at 10:30 Eastern, except in holiday
  weeks. On Friday evening the latest public level is normally the one of the
  previous Friday.
- **Forecast origin**: Friday 18:00 Eastern.

## Audit findings

### NOAA forecasts

- One product line throughout (`Daily Heating Degree Days Forecast Based on
  NDFD Data Issued 0000UTC`), always 7 days plus a total, always `Weights:
  Utility Gas`. The only change is cosmetic: `Regions: Regions::CensusDivisions`
  in the first 225 files, `CPC::Regions::CensusDivisions` afterwards.
- No change of weighting: regressing CONUS on the nine divisions year by year
  gives the same weights from 2014 to 2026 (residual 0.28 HDD, the integer
  rounding). CPC states the weights come from the 2010 Census / American
  Community Survey.
- Missing data: 65 days without a file (HTTP 404) and 2 empty files;
  `-9999` marks missing cells, in 64 entire files and 63 partially (mostly
  the last day). 4,513 of the 4,707 issue days are complete. Where present,
  the `Total` column always equals the sum of the seven days.
  Among winter issue Fridays (November to March, 2014/15 to 2025/26): 5 without
  a file (2022-01-07, 2023-12-29, 2024-01-12, 2024-02-09, 2024-03-08), 3
  entirely `-9999` (2018-11-02, 2022-12-30, 2025-03-21) and 4 without the last
  day.
- Posting time: 4,450 files carry the issue day as `Last-Modified` (about
  15:00 UTC in winter, 14:00 UTC in summer, i.e. 10:00 Eastern). 191 were
  rewritten in four batches (`posting = batch_rewrite`): 2014-01-01 to
  2014-03-12 (written 2014-03-13), 2019-07-01 to 07-07, 2019-10-06 to 10-16
  and 2020-01-01 to 2020-04-11 (written 2020-04-12); one more file
  (2014-11-30) is dated the next day. The 2020 batch covers 13 winter Fridays.
  Its errors still grow with the forecast horizon like other years (day-6
  standard deviation 1.97 HDD in Jan–Mar 2020 against 2.08–3.03 in other
  years), so it does not look contaminated by observations, but it cannot be
  verified: results are also reported without these weeks.
- Forecasts are too warm on average (+0.3 HDD per day nationally) and have
  improved over time (day-6 error standard deviation about 3.0 HDD in
  2015–2017, 2.1–2.4 in 2023–2026): error distributions must come from recent
  windows.

### NOAA observations

Complete daily series since 2013-01-01, no `-9999`. Three archived copies
(January 2017, December 2024, March 2025) are identical to the current files
on every common cell: no revisions detected, observations are treated as
real-time with a two-day lag.

### EIA storage

- Latest vintage: 881 consecutive Fridays from 2010-01-01.
- Original estimates exist for every week since 1993: `archrevisions.xls` for
  the three-region structure (to 2015-08-28) and `revisions.xls` for the
  five-region structure (from 2015-06-19).
- Structure switch: reports used three regions up to the week ending
  2015-11-06 (released 2015-11-12) and five regions from the week ending
  2015-11-13. The five-region "original" values for June–November 2015 are
  reallocations, not what was published at the time; first-release values
  before the switch come from the three-region files.
- Revisions affect 194 weekly levels. Over the 259 winter issue Fridays of the
  experiment, the first-release and latest-vintage targets differ in 37 weeks,
  by 3 Bcf or more in 13 and by at most 5 Bcf; revisions mostly move volume
  between adjacent weeks (for instance +5 and -5 Bcf on the weeks starting
  2016-02-05 and 2016-02-12). A 33-week restatement (2025-08-29 to 2026-04-10)
  lowered levels by about 10 Bcf with little effect on weekly changes. Some
  weeks were revised twice (2026-03-27: 1,865, then 1,861, then 1,851 Bcf).
- Not recoverable: intermediate vintages and unpublished revisions below the
  4 Bcf publication threshold. First-release targets are therefore
  pseudo-real-time to within a few Bcf.
- EIA's sampling standard error of the weekly Lower-48 change is about
  1.2 Bcf.

### EIA release calendar

Holiday exceptions are read from every archived copy of the schedule page
(51 distinct contents in the Wayback Machine, April 2010 to June 2026) plus
the live page retrieved at the freeze.
For each holiday the most recent copy listing it wins, because EIA updates
entries after the fact. Two entries changed: the report for the week ending
2019-06-28, announced for Friday 2019-07-05, came out on Wednesday
2019-07-03; the one for the week ending 2025-12-19, announced for Wednesday
2025-12-24, came out on Monday 2025-12-29. A holiday replaces the Thursday
release of its Monday–Sunday week (after federal observance of weekend
holidays); the reference Friday is six days earlier. Older copies give the
holiday's date; newer ones only its name, so the date follows the federal
rule (and, for the 2025 National Day of Mourning, the executive order).

The result is 45 exceptions for weeks from 2013-11-22 to 2027-11-19, including
two national days of mourning (5 December 2018, 9 January 2025). Only one
report was released after the following Friday evening: the week ending
2025-12-19, so on Friday 2025-12-26 the latest public level was that of
2025-12-12. The calendar starts with the week ending 2013-11-01: earlier
copies include an "EIA Closed" entry for the October 2013 shutdown that does
not identify the week, and the C++ calendar refuses earlier weeks. Unscheduled
delays that never appeared on the schedule page cannot be recovered. One
archived copy spells "Veterns Day"; the parser accepts it.

## Information set and target (C++, `include/ccold/realtime/info_set.hpp`)

At the origin, Friday `t` at 18:00 Eastern:

- **Forecast.** Each of the 70 cells (10 regions x 7 days, `t` to `t+6`) takes
  its value from the freshest usable bulletin, at most two days old. A
  bulletin is usable if its file is dated before the origin; batch-rewritten
  files are accepted only under `AcceptBatchRewrites` (assumed available from
  16:00 UTC on their issue day) and flag the information set as
  pseudo-real-time. A missing Friday file is thus replaced by Thursday's
  bulletin, which covers six of the seven days.
- **Observations**: days up to `t-2`.
- **EIA**: the latest report released before the origin (normally the week
  ending `t-7`), first-release level and the net withdrawal printed in that
  report. Revisions are never used in real time, because when they became
  public is not recorded.
- **Target**: `S(t) - S(t+7)` from first releases, public when the report for
  `t+7` comes out; the latest vintage is kept for checks. For `t = 2015-11-06`
  the start level is the five-region restatement (3,985 Bcf) that the first
  five-region report used, so the target is -15 Bcf rather than -22.
- **Training targets** at an origin are those whose release time precedes it;
  a property test checks every Friday from 2014 to 2026 under both policies.

Winter issue Fridays (November to March, 2014/15 to 2025/26): 259, of which 247
have a complete 70-cell forecast. The 12 incomplete ones are 2018-03-09,
2018-12-28, 2022-11-04, 2022-11-11, 2022-12-30, 2023-12-29, 2024-01-12,
2024-02-09 (last day missing), 2022-01-07, 2024-03-08 (two days missing),
2018-11-02 and 2025-03-21 (no forecast: three consecutive `-9999` days). Under
the strict policy the 13 Fridays of January–March 2020 also have no forecast
(234 complete). The C++ pipeline reproduces the feasibility audit: on the 247
complete Fridays, regressing the first-release target on the forecast
national HDD and a trend gives 1.77 Bcf per HDD and a residual standard
deviation of 26.2 Bcf (audit: 1.78 and 25.8 on a slightly different sample).

### Pseudo-real-time elements

1. EIA first-release values (intermediate vintages unobservable).
2. NOAA bulletins rewritten in batches after issue (Jan–Mar 2014, Jan–Apr 2020).
3. Only the 00 UTC NDFD issue is archived; by Friday evening a newer issue
   exists that this dataset does not see, so the information set is
   conservative.
4. Release-calendar entries confirmed only by copies taken before the release
   date (`last_snapshot_after_release = 0`).
